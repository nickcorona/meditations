# Principles

Principles worth keeping, most useful first: Marcus Aurelius (quoted from book.md, under the passage number) and a few other books. Each has `when:` (the moment it applies) and `eg:` (a concrete case), because a rule only helps if I notice I'm in its situation. Entries from other books also have `from:`; public-domain ones are quoted exactly, the rest are paraphrased and say so. Cue Home shows the when and the line; a tap opens the example (and, for Marcus, the full passage).

## 8.22a
when: I'm pushing a task to tomorrow
eg: Booking the movers is on Home again. Send the first email now, not tomorrow.
You could be good today. But instead you choose tomorrow.

## clear-1
from: James Clear, Atomic Habits (paraphrased)
when: a habit feels too big to start
eg: "Go for a run" becomes "put on running shoes."
Make it so easy you can't say no: start with a version that takes two minutes.

## 5.1
when: I'm lying in bed not wanting to start the day
eg: The alarm goes and I want ten more minutes. Feet on the floor: the day's work is what I'm for.
I have to go to work—as a human being.

## harris-3
from: Russ Harris, The Happiness Trap (paraphrased)
when: I'm waiting to feel ready before I act
eg: Waiting to feel confident before messaging a match. Send it while nervous.
Fighting a feeling makes it bigger. Make room for it and keep moving toward what matters.

## epictetus-50
from: Epictetus, Enchiridion 50
when: I keep saying I'll start properly later
eg: "I'll get serious about the job search after the move." Start this week.
How long, then, will you delay to demand of yourself the noblest improvements?

## burkeman-4
from: Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks (paraphrased)
when: I'm waiting until things settle down
eg: "After the move I'll start..." Start now.
Life never arrives fully under control. Act now, in imperfect conditions.

## seneca-1
from: Seneca, On the Shortness of Life
when: I'm telling myself there's no time
eg: "No time to work on applications" after two hours on YouTube.
We do not have a very short time assigned to us, but we lose a great deal of it.

## seneca-2
from: Seneca, On the Shortness of Life
when: I'm getting through the week until something later
eg: Counting down to Friday, or to after the move.
Postponement is the greatest waste of life: it wrings day after day from us, and takes away the present by promising something hereafter.

## 8.47
when: I'm upset about something that happened
eg: A match unmatches me. The sting is my story about what it means, not the event.
External things are not the problem. It’s your assessment of them.

## harris-1
from: Russ Harris, The Happiness Trap (paraphrased)
when: a strong feeling is pushing me to act
eg: Anxious before a hard conversation at work. "I'm noticing anxiety", then have the conversation.
Feelings are information, not orders. Notice the feeling, name it, then choose what to do.

## burns-5
from: David Burns, Feeling Good (paraphrased)
when: I feel something, so it seems true
eg: "I feel like a failure, so I am one."
Feelings aren't facts. Feeling hopeless doesn't make things hopeless.

## epictetus-1
from: Epictetus, Enchiridion 1
when: I'm stewing over something I'm waiting on
eg: Waiting to hear back on an application. Sending it was mine; their decision isn't.
There are things which are within our power, and there are things which are beyond our power.

## 8.49
when: I'm reading meaning into a text or a silence
eg: She hasn't replied in a day. That's all I know, not that she's lost interest.
Stick with first impressions. Don’t extrapolate.

## burns-1
from: David Burns, Feeling Good (paraphrased)
when: I'm assuming what someone thinks of me
eg: "My manager thinks I'm slow", with no evidence. Ask, or look at what they've actually said.
Mind reading is a distortion: you're assuming what others think without evidence. Check before you believe it.

## burns-3
from: David Burns, Feeling Good (paraphrased)
when: I'm predicting something will go badly
eg: "This interview is going to go terribly." That's a forecast, not a fact.
Fortune-telling treats a prediction as a fact. Ask what the evidence actually is.

## duke-2
from: Annie Duke, Thinking in Bets (paraphrased)
when: I'm certain about something that's actually uncertain
eg: "She's definitely not interested." Would I bet $100 on that?
Ask "Want to bet?" How sure am I, really, as a percentage?

## harris-4
from: Russ Harris, The Happiness Trap (paraphrased)
when: I'm angry or anxious about something
eg: Irritated that the landlord keeps stalling. What I care about is a reliable home; act on that.
Ask what the feeling is pointing at: what that matters to me is at stake here?

## 5.20
when: a plan falls through
eg: The elevator's taken on move day. The work now is another slot or another way, not the old plan.
The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.

## duke-1
from: Annie Duke, Thinking in Bets (paraphrased)
when: I'm judging a decision by how it turned out
eg: The apartment I picked had a leak. Was it a good decision given what I knew then?
Don't judge a decision by its outcome. Judge it by the process and the information you had.

## duke-4
from: Annie Duke, Thinking in Bets (paraphrased)
when: I'm drawing a lesson from one result
eg: A good interview and no offer. Was it skill, or luck?
Outcomes are skill plus luck. Separate the two before you learn anything from one.

## clear-4
from: James Clear, Atomic Habits (paraphrased)
when: I've missed a day
eg: Skipped the gym yesterday. Today is non-negotiable.
Never miss twice. One slip is an accident; two is the start of a new habit.

## burns-2
from: David Burns, Feeling Good (paraphrased)
when: one slip makes the whole thing feel ruined
eg: One off-plan dinner and "the cut is blown." It's one meal in a month.
All-or-nothing thinking: it's rarely a total failure. Look for the shades of grey.

## 4.24
when: I'm overloaded or about to say yes to something
eg: A meeting invite with no agenda. Ask whether it's needed before accepting.
Ask yourself at every moment, “Is this necessary?”

## burkeman-1
from: Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks (paraphrased)
when: the to-do list feels impossible
eg: Move week. Choose which tasks simply won't happen.
You'll never get through everything. Decide what to neglect on purpose.

## 8.36
when: everything feels huge at once
eg: Move, job search and the cut all pile up on Sunday night. Pick the one thing for tomorrow morning.
Don’t let your imagination be crushed by life as a whole.

## harris-2
from: Russ Harris, The Happiness Trap (paraphrased)
when: a harsh thought about myself keeps repeating
eg: "I'm going to mess up this presentation" becomes "I'm having the thought that I'll mess it up."
Notice the thought as a thought. Saying "I'm having the thought that..." loosens its grip.

## burns-4
from: David Burns, Feeling Good (paraphrased)
when: I'm talking to myself harshly
eg: "I'm such an idiot" after a mistake in a meeting. I'd never say that to a friend.
Talk to yourself the way you'd talk to a friend in the same situation.

## 2.1
when: someone's being rude, careless or dishonest with me
eg: A coworker takes credit for my analysis in a meeting. They're grabbing what looks good to them, not attacking me.
They are like this because they can’t tell good from evil.

## seneca-3
from: Seneca, On Anger
when: I feel wronged by someone
eg: A friend bails on plans at the last minute. If they're good, there's probably a reason; if not, it's no surprise.
Is it a good man who has wronged you? do not believe it: is it a bad one? do not be surprised.

## epictetus-20
from: Epictetus, Enchiridion 20
when: someone provokes me and I want to fire back
eg: A snippy Slack message. Wait an hour before replying.
For if you once gain time and respite, you will more easily command yourself.

## voss-1
from: Chris Voss, Never Split the Difference (paraphrased)
when: a conversation is getting tense
eg: The landlord pushes back on a repair. "It sounds like you're worried about the cost."
Label the emotion out loud: "It seems like..." Naming what they feel takes the heat out of it.

## voss-2
from: Chris Voss, Never Split the Difference (paraphrased)
when: someone makes an offer that doesn't work for me
eg: The movers quote too high. "How am I supposed to make that work?"
Ask "how" and "what" questions and let them solve your problem, instead of splitting the difference.

## voss-3
from: Chris Voss, Never Split the Difference (paraphrased)
when: I want someone to agree with me
eg: Pitching a modelling change to a stakeholder. Sum up their concern until they say "That's right."
Aim for "That's right", not "yes". Summarize their view so well they confirm it.

## 6.6
when: I want to get back at someone
eg: Someone's passive-aggressive in Slack. Reply plainly; don't match their tone.
The best revenge is not to be like that.

## 12.4
when: I'm replaying what someone thinks of me
eg: Worried a VP thought my update was weak. Would I trust their read on my work over my own?
We all love ourselves more than other people, but care more about their opinion than our own.

## manson-3
from: Mark Manson, Models (paraphrased)
when: I keep checking whether she's replied
eg: Refreshing the app. Go do something I care about.
Neediness is caring more about their approval than your own life. Invest in your life first.

## clear-2
from: James Clear, Atomic Habits (paraphrased)
when: I keep failing at the same thing
eg: Snacking at night. Don't keep it in the apartment, instead of trying to resist it.
You don't rise to your goals; you fall to your systems. Fix the setup, not the willpower.

## clear-3
from: James Clear, Atomic Habits (paraphrased)
when: a small choice feels like it doesn't matter
eg: One more set or skip it. It's a vote either way.
Every action is a vote for the type of person you want to become.

## 6.2
when: I'm tired, cold or not in the mood to do what I planned
eg: Wiped after work with the gym on the plan. Go anyway; the mood isn't part of the deal.
Just that you do the right thing. The rest doesn’t matter.

## 10.16
when: I'm reading or planning about getting better instead of doing it
eg: A third article on discipline tonight. Close it and do the two-minute task.
To stop talking about what the good man is like, and just be one.

## epictetus-13
from: Epictetus, Enchiridion 13
when: I'm avoiding something new because I'll look bad at it
eg: A new lift at the gym or a beginner class. Looking clumsy is the price of getting better.
If you would improve, be content to be thought foolish and dull with regard to externals.

## 6.19
when: I'm telling myself I can't do it because it's hard
eg: The spatial model won't converge. Hard isn't impossible; people do this.
Not to assume it’s impossible because you find it hard. But to recognize that if it’s humanly possible, you can do it too.

## manson-1
from: Mark Manson, Models (paraphrased)
when: I'm hiding my interest to stay safe
eg: Hedging instead of saying "I'd like to see you again." Say it.
State your interest honestly and let them respond. A no just means you're not a match.

## manson-2
from: Mark Manson, Models (paraphrased)
when: I'm watering myself down to be liked
eg: A bland bio or opener written to offend no one.
Show who you really are. Turning some people off is how the right ones are drawn in.

## manson-4
from: Mark Manson, Models (paraphrased)
when: a date or message didn't go the way I hoped
eg: She said no after I asked her out. I asked honestly; that was the win.
Judge yourself by whether you acted honestly, not by the outcome.

## 8.50
when: something's bad and I'm complaining instead of acting
eg: The bedroom's noisy at night. Buy earplugs or move the bed; don't stew.
The cucumber is bitter? Then throw it out. There are brambles in the path? Then go around them.

## 5.16
when: I notice I've been chewing on the same thought
eg: An hour rehearsing a grievance. It's colouring the whole day; switch tracks.
Your soul takes on the color of your thoughts.

## 6.21
when: I'm getting defensive about being wrong
eg: Someone finds a bug in my code. Thank them; the fix makes my work better.
It’s the truth I’m after, and the truth never harmed anyone.

## 7.7
when: I'm stuck and don't want to ask
eg: An hour stuck on a deploy. Ask someone now.
Don’t be ashamed to need help.

## clear-5
from: James Clear, Atomic Habits (paraphrased)
when: I keep falling into the same distraction
eg: Phone keeps pulling me off work. Put it in another room.
Make the bad habit hard: put friction between you and the cue.

## newport-1
from: Cal Newport, Digital Minimalism (paraphrased)
when: I reach for my phone out of boredom
eg: Waiting in line at the store. Leave the phone in my pocket.
Practice solitude: let your mind be alone with its own thoughts instead of filling every gap.

## newport-3
from: Cal Newport, Digital Minimalism (paraphrased)
when: the evening's empty and I'm scrolling
eg: 9pm on the couch. Cook something, lift, read, or go out.
Replace low-quality leisure with demanding, real-world activity.

## burkeman-2
from: Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks (paraphrased)
when: I'm saving the important work for later in the day
eg: Job applications before email and Slack.
Do the thing that matters most first, before the day gets its hands on you.

## grove-1
from: Andy Grove, High Output Management (paraphrased)
when: I'm choosing what to work on as a manager
eg: Fixing a dashboard myself versus unblocking three analysts.
A manager's output is the output of the team. Do the things with the most leverage.

## grove-2
from: Andy Grove, High Output Management (paraphrased)
when: I'm going into a 1:1
eg: Weekly 1:1 with an analyst. Let them set the agenda; ask and listen.
The 1:1 is the report's meeting. They bring the agenda; you ask questions and listen.

## grove-3
from: Andy Grove, High Output Management (paraphrased)
when: I'm handing off a task
eg: Handing a model review to a junior. Check in more often than with a senior.
Delegating without following up is abdication. Match how closely you check to their experience with the task.

## grove-4
from: Andy Grove, High Output Management (paraphrased)
when: someone on the team isn't performing
eg: An analyst's work keeps slipping. Is it can't (skill) or won't (motivation)?
When someone isn't performing, it's either can't or won't. Find out which before you act.

## burkeman-3
from: Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks (paraphrased)
when: I'm juggling too many projects
eg: Three side projects half done. Pick one to finish before touching the others.
Limit work in progress: finish one big thing before starting another.

## newport-2
from: Cal Newport, Digital Minimalism (paraphrased)
when: I'm tempted by a new app or service
eg: A new productivity app. Does it serve something I deeply value, better than what I have?
Use a technology only if it's the best way to support something you deeply value.

## newport-4
from: Cal Newport, Digital Minimalism (paraphrased)
when: I'm about to like or react instead of reaching out
eg: Reacting to a friend's story. Text them properly instead.
A like isn't connection. Have the actual conversation.

## burkeman-5
from: Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks (paraphrased)
when: I can't choose because something better might come along
eg: An apartment or a date that's good, not perfect.
Commit to the option in front of you instead of keeping every door open.

## duke-3
from: Annie Duke, Thinking in Bets (paraphrased)
when: I'm stuck between options
eg: Two job offers. Picture myself a year from now looking back.
Imagine your future self looking back: which choice will you wish you'd made?

## 2.5
when: I'm half-doing something while my mind's elsewhere
eg: Checking Slack during a 1:1. Phone face down, full attention.
Do everything as if it were the last thing you were doing in your life.

## 7.69
when: I'm rushing, slacking or putting on a front
eg: Frantic before a deadline or coasting after it. Steady pace, no show.
Perfection of character: to live your last day, every day, without frenzy, or sloth, or pretense.

## 4.17
when: I'm putting off doing something decent until later
eg: Meaning to text a friend who's having a hard time. Send it today.
While you’re alive and able—be good.

## 2.11
when: I'm acting like there's unlimited time
eg: Holding a grudge for weeks. Would I keep it if this were my last month?
You could leave life right now. Let that determine what you do and say and think.

## 12.17
when: I'm tempted to shade the truth or cut a corner
eg: Tempted to round up a model's lift in a deck. Show the real number.
If it’s not right, don’t do it. If it’s not true, don’t say it.

## 4.49a
when: something went wrong and I'm calling it bad luck
eg: Losing a deposit. Handling it well is the part I get to win.
The thing itself was no misfortune at all; to endure it and prevail is great good fortune.

## 6.51
when: my mood depends on how others react
eg: Checking who reacted to my post. Judge the day by what I did.
Sanity means tying it to your own actions.

## 7.71
when: someone else's flaws are grating on me
eg: Irritated a coworker is always late. Work on my own habit of overpromising instead.
It’s silly to try to escape other people’s faults. They are inescapable. Just try to escape your own.

## 8.59
when: someone keeps frustrating me
eg: A stakeholder misreads the model again. Explain it once more, or let it go.
People exist for one another. You can instruct or endure them.

## 6.53
when: I'm waiting for my turn to talk
eg: In a debate about methodology. Say their point back before answering.
Practice really hearing what people say. Do your best to get inside their minds.

## 7.15
when: someone's behaviour makes me want to drop my standards
eg: A rude email. Still reply well.
No matter what anyone says or does, my task is to be good.

## 10.3
when: I'm thinking I can't take any more
eg: A hard week of the cut and the move. It's endurable if I treat it as temporary and chosen.
You can endure anything your mind can make endurable.

## 11.18
when: I'm tempted to be snarky or cold
eg: Someone's hostile in a review. A kind, specific answer disarms more than sarcasm.
Kindness is invincible, provided it’s sincere—not ironic or an act.

## 5.9
when: I'm replaying a slip
eg: Ate off-plan last night. Back on today, no punishment.
To get back up when you fail, to celebrate behaving like a human.

## 7.59
when: I feel like there's nothing good in me today
eg: A flat, irritable day. One decent act gets it going.
Dig deep; the water—goodness—is down there. And as long as you keep digging, it will keep bubbling up.

## 6.30
when: I notice I'm drifting from who I mean to be
eg: Third night of scrolling late. That's not who I'm building.
Fight to be the person philosophy tried to make you.

## 4.32
when: I'm spending too long on something small
eg: Forty minutes on a slide's layout. Ship it.
You’re better off not giving the small things more time than they deserve.

## 3.10
when: I'm living in the past or the future
eg: Rehearsing last week's conversation. Only this moment is live.
Each of us lives only now, this brief instant.

## 11.13
when: someone's contempt is getting to me
eg: A match is rude after I say no. Not mine to fix; mine is not answering in kind.
Someone despises me. That’s their problem. Mine: not to do or say anything despicable.

## 7.73
when: I'm annoyed nobody noticed or said thanks
eg: Helped a teammate debug for an hour, no thanks. The help was the point.
You’ve given aid and they’ve received it. And yet, like an idiot, you keep holding out for more: to be credited with a Good Deed, to be repaid in kind. Why?

## epictetus-46
from: Epictetus, Enchiridion 46
when: I want to tell people about the changes I'm making
eg: Announcing the cut at work. Let the results show instead.
Sheep do not hastily throw up the grass to show the shepherds how much they have eaten, but, inwardly digesting their food, they produce it outwardly in wool and milk.

## harris-5
from: Russ Harris, The Happiness Trap (paraphrased)
when: I'm not sure what to do next
eg: A free Saturday morning with no plan. Pick what serves health or the job search.
Choose by values, not by mood: what would the person I want to be do right now?
